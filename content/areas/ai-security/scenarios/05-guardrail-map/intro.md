> **In plain terms.** A building fire plan puts an extinguisher, a door and an alarm at each point on the escape route, not all of them in the lobby. The guardrail map does the same for the agent: a control on every hop from the user's question to the email leaving.
>
> *Where the analogy breaks:* fire does not change its route when it sees your plan. Attackers do, so the map is reviewed every time a new tool or data source is added.

You have exploited the agent four ways and defended it each time. This last
scenario has no attack. Its job is to compress everything into a **map you can
redraw from memory** — because that is what actually makes you employable in AI
security: not "I ran a tool," but "I can design the controls."

The frame: draw the data path from the user's question to a real side effect, and
put a named control on every hop. If you can do that on a whiteboard, you
understand this Area.
